Capability / Logo and identity

Brand identity and logo design in Miami

A logo on its own is not an identity. An identity is the mark, its variants, the palette, the type system and the rules for using them, drawn together so the brand still looks like itself on a shopfront, a phone screen and a Google listing.

What you actually receive

The mark, and everything around it

Most logo work ends at a file. That is where the problems start: the mark that looked right in the presentation falls apart at 24 pixels, has no version for a dark background, and sits next to a typeface nobody chose.

01 The mark Primary lockup, plus the variants a real business needs: stacked, horizontal, mark alone, one colour, reversed.
02 Palette A working palette with the ratios that make it hold together, not a row of swatches.
03 Type system A display face and a text face, with the sizes and weights each one is for.
04 Art direction How photography, layout and spacing should behave, so the next thing you make still fits.
05 Files Vector and raster, in the formats a printer, a sign maker and a social platform each ask for.
06 Guidelines Short and usable. The rules that matter, not forty pages nobody opens.

Drawn against where it will live

Not on a white square

An identity is tested by the worst place it appears, not the best. A mark is drawn here against the awning, the Instagram avatar, the invoice and the favicon in the same pass, because those are the sizes and grounds where a weak one gives out.

Renata Estrella is the clearest example. The monogram, the palette and the type system were drawn from nothing, on a near-black ground chosen so the gold reads as metal rather than as yellow, and the same system carries into the e-book and the Google listing without being redrawn.

When the mark already exists

Art direction without a redraw

Not every project needs a new logo. Sometimes the mark is fine and everything around it is missing, which is a cheaper and faster problem to solve.

Blend Hair Boutique came with their own script wordmark. What did not exist was the system underneath it: the ground, the palette, the type pairing and the rules for how photography behaves. That work is art direction, and it is quoted separately from identity design for exactly that reason.
